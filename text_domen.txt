  <p class="lede">A cell owns a territory of one to four nodes. Shedding frees nodes, neighbours claim them and spread, tension rises, reserve cells wake and divide, and division splits the territory, returning tension to normal.</p>

  <p class="note"><b>Finding a circadian-lethal regime.</b> The reverted lineage can only claim a niche or start dividing while its own local glucose (readout below) sits at or above this gate, and its glycogen refill scales with the same local glucose — it has no counter-regulatory buffer, unlike the normal lineage.   <h2>What the run showed</h2>
  <p class="note"><b>An exact feasibility condition falls out of these numbers.</b> To offset shedding, every departing cell must be replaced by a division. Only reserve cells divide and each division takes one cycle time, so the throughput of the reserve is <code>D / T</code> divisions per hour while the requirement is <code>F / τ</code>. Setting them equal gives</p>
  <p class="note" style="text-align:center;font-size:14px"><code>reserve fraction ≥ T<sub>cycle</sub> / τ</code></p>
  <p class="note">With a 15 h cycle and a 6 day lifespan this is 15/144 = <b>10.4 %</b> — exactly the 10 % specified. The tissue sits right on the feasibility boundary, with zero margin.</p>

  <p class="note">The transition is sharp. At τ = 6 days the reserve is 98 % mobilized, yet the sheet still fails to hold its density and settles at 52 %, with cells living permanently stretched twofold. At 9 days the margin is already 1.4-fold and the density 97 %. So this tissue describes not a healthy epithelium but a maximally loaded one: it has nothing left to heal a wound with, as the entire reserve goes into routine turnover.</p>
  <p class="note">Check this with the τ slider on the bench: sweep it from 6 to 12 days and watch the reserve margin and the density. The reverse sweep is just as informative — that is what exhaustion of the reserve under accelerated turnover looks like.</p>

  <h2>Conclusions</h2>
  <p class="note"><b>1. Feasibility condition.</b> <code>reserve fraction ≥ T<sub>cycle</sub> / τ</code>. At a 15 h cycle and a 6 day lifespan this is 10.4 %, i.e. exactly the 10 % specified — the tissue sits on the boundary with zero margin.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>2. The boundary is sharp.</b> At τ = 6 d the reserve is 98 % mobilized and density still collapses to 52 %; at τ = 9 d the margin is 1.44× and density 97 %. Three days of lifespan separate a healthy sheet from an exhausted one.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>3. Healing has two beats.</b> Coverage runs on the spreading clock (minutes to hours), density restoration on the cell-cycle clock (days). Prediction: the metabolic crisis, if it occurs at all, arrives <i>after</i> the wound looks closed — in the phase of a thin, spread sheet.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>4. Small defects close without cell migration</b>, by convergence of the edges. Division only joins on the second beat.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>5. A flat monolayer cannot starve, so the regeneration pendulum cannot switch on in it.</b> Supply enters per unit area and consumers sit per unit area, so per-cell supply is independent of sheet size. With no deficit there is no amplitude. The model confirms this behaviourally: it converges either to homeostasis or to a chronically stretched sheet, but it never oscillates. Two further arms of the loop are also broken in a single layer: cell loss is vented apically, away from the basement membrane, and the tight junctions seal beneath the departing cell before it leaves, so released MMPs never reach the basal compartment. In stratified epithelium all four arms close, because basal cells are anchored by hemidesmosomes, cannot be extruded apically, and die in place against the membrane — while thickness makes per-cell supply fall as 1/n.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>6. Methodological.</b> The mortality produced by the earlier lattice version with migration was an artefact of representation: a departing cell moved a hole instead of covering area. The territory model removes it by construction.</p>

  <p class="note"><b>Empirical test of conclusion 5 — and it only half survives.</b> Read literally as "simple epithelium", the prediction fails: most common carcinomas arise from single-layered epithelia — colorectal, gastric, endometrial, pancreatic ductal, lung adenocarcinoma, renal cell, thyroid, cholangiocarcinoma, tubal/ovarian serous. Read strictly as "flat monolayer on a free surface", the prediction holds strikingly: endothelium, mesothelium, alveolar type I cells and glomerular podocytes together cover an enormous area yet almost never give rise to carcinoma, and where they do (mesothelioma) it takes decades of asbestos injury. The discriminating variable therefore is not the number of layers but <b>whether the proliferative compartment is spatially concentrated relative to its supply</b>: dispersed division on a flat sheet creates no local demand hotspot, whereas a crypt base, a gland, or a stratified basal layer does. Caveat: the comparison is confounded, since the cancer-resistant flat epithelia are also slow-turnover and unexposed to luminal carcinogens, so low incidence has at least two other sufficient explanations.</p>

  <div class="rule"></div>

  <p class="note"><b>What changed in the core.</b> Previously a lattice node was a cell, so area could only be covered by division — hence the order-of-magnitude mismatch against real tissue and all the spurious migration-driven mortality. Now a cell owns a territory and coverage proceeds by two fast processes that create no new consumers: <b>spreading</b>, where a free node goes to the least stretched neighbour, and <b>shift</b>, where a node passes from a more stretched cell to a less stretched one so that tension redistributes across the sheet. Division remains a slow process on cell-cycle time and now only <i>restores density</i> rather than closing the hole.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>Tension is area.</b> A cell on one node is relaxed; on four it is stretched to the limit. The tension a cell senses is averaged over itself and its neighbours, with an adjacent empty node counted as maximal stretch. A reserve cell wakes when the averaged tension crosses the threshold and it holds at least two nodes to divide between. Division hands half the territory to the daughter, and tension drops for both. That is the whole loop.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>The mother stays in reserve.</b> Otherwise, at a 1:10 ratio the reserve pool drains within a day: each division would spend one reserve cell and return 0.09. So division here is asymmetric — the mother returns to the reserve, and the daughter joins the reserve with probability 1/11, otherwise differentiating. The pool then grows slowly, bounded by the reserve cells' own lifespan.</p>
  <p class="note"><b>On terminology.</b> «Reserve cells» here means reversibly quiescent, division-competent cells of the normal sheet. The word <i>dormancy</i> is avoided on purpose: in the literature it usually denotes disseminated tumour cells arrested at a distant site, which is a different state with a different mechanism.</p>
  <p class="note">Borders are drawn around cells rather than nodes, so a spread cell appears as a large polygon. The field is 76 × 79 nodes, one node is 20 µm, and one step is 3 minutes.         } else {

          // free node is reclaimed within one step, so an instantaneous test
          // kills every reversion before it can act. Tolerance = 0 restores the
          // literal rule.
          if(--ctol[id]<=0){ dying.push(id); revLost++; continue; }
        }
      }

      // instantaneous glucose level (that is the counter-regulatory buffer
      // it has and the reverted lineage does not); the reverted lineage's
      // refill is gated by the same local glucose that gates its growth, so

        clin[kid]=1; cst[kid]=DIFF; clife[kid]=1e9; ctol[kid]=tolSteps();
        cgly[kid]=sMax0*S.revStore; revTotal++;
        cst[id]=RESV; cprog[id]=0; cage[id]=0;
      } else {
        clin[kid]=0;
        cst[kid]= rnd()<S.pResv?RESV:DIFF;
        clife[kid]= cst[kid]===RESV?resvLifeH:lifeH+(rnd()-.5)*2*lifeSp;
        cst[id]=RESV; cprog[id]=0; cage[id]=0;
      }
      divTotal++;
    }
  }

  if(dying.length){
    for(const id of dying) dyFlag[id]=1;
    for(let i=0;i<N;i++){ const a=owner[i]; if(a>=0&&dyFlag[a]) owner[i]=-1; }
    for(const id of dying){ dyFlag[id]=0; if(calive[id]) killCell(id); }
    dying.length=0;
  }

  const c=count();
  if(++tr.acc>=tr.every){
    tr.acc=0;
    tr.dens[tr.head]=c.cells/N; tr.div[tr.head]=c.nv;
    tr.resv[tr.head]=c.nd; tr.g[tr.head]=c.gmin;
    tr.time[tr.head]=step*K.dtMin/60;
    tr.head=(tr.head+1)%TRN;
    if(tr.n<TRN) tr.n++; else tr.every*=2;
  }
  return c;
}

/* largest contiguous patch of reverted cells, measured in cells not nodes */
const seenN=new Uint8Array(N), stackN=new Int32Array(N), seenC=new Uint8Array(cap);
function largestClone(){
  seenN.fill(0);
  let best=0;
  for(let s0=0;s0<N;s0++){
    const a0=owner[s0];
    if(seenN[s0]||a0<0||clin[a0]!==1) continue;
    let sp=0, cells=0;
    stackN[sp++]=s0; seenN[s0]=1;
    const touched=[];
    while(sp){
      const i=stackN[--sp], a=owner[i];
      if(!seenC[a]){ seenC[a]=1; touched.push(a); cells++; }
      const b=i*6;
      for(let d=0;d<6;d++){
        const q=NBF[b+d]; if(q<0||seenN[q]) continue;
        const bb=owner[q]; if(bb<0||clin[bb]!==1) continue;
        seenN[q]=1; stackN[sp++]=q;
      }
    }
    for(const a of touched) seenC[a]=0;
    if(cells>best) best=cells;
  }
  return best;
}

function count(){
  let nd=0,nf=0,nv=0,ns=0,free=0,gmin=1,asum=0,ac=0;
  for(let i=0;i<N;i++){ if(owner[i]<0) free++; else if(g[i]<gmin) gmin=g[i]; }
  let rev=0,revR=0;
  for(let id=0;id<nextId;id++){
    if(!calive[id]) continue;
    const s=cst[id];
    if(clin[id]===1){ if(s===RESV)revR++; else if(s!==SHED)rev++; }
    else { if(s===RESV)nd++;else if(s===DIFF)nf++;else if(s===DIV)nv++;else ns++; }
    asum+=carea[id]; ac++;
  }
  const norm=nd+nf+nv+ns;
  return {nd,nf,nv,ns,free,gmin,cells:ac,aMean:ac?asum/ac:1,rev,revR,norm,
          dens:ac/N, mob: (nd+nv)?nv/(nd+nv):0, clone:largestClone()};
}

function drawTrace(){
  const {ctx,w,h}=fit(traceCv,.34);
  ctx.fillStyle='rgba(255,255,255,.5)'; ctx.fillRect(0,0,w,h);
  const pL=30,pR=32,pT=8,pB=14,iw=w-pL-pR,ih=h-pT-pB;
  ctx.strokeStyle='rgba(27,42,46,.12)'; ctx.lineWidth=.5; ctx.beginPath();
  for(let i=0;i<=4;i++){const y=pT+ih*i/4;ctx.moveTo(pL,y);ctx.lineTo(w-pR,y);} ctx.stroke();
  const n=tr.n,start=(tr.head-n+TRN)%TRN;
  let mx=40;
  for(let i=0;i<n;i++){const j=(start+i)%TRN; if(tr.div[j]>mx)mx=tr.div[j]; if(tr.resv[j]>mx)mx=tr.resv[j];}
  // dynamic window: the x-axis spans exactly the recorded period, not a fixed
  // buffer width, so the trace always fills the plate at whatever timescale
  // is currently being recorded
  const tMin = n? tr.time[start] : 0;
  const tMax = n? tr.time[(tr.head-1+TRN)%TRN] : 0;
  const tSpan = Math.max(1e-6, tMax-tMin);
  const px=i=>{ if(n<2) return pL;
    const j=(start+i)%TRN; return pL+iw*(tr.time[j]-tMin)/tSpan; };
  const line=(a,sc,col,wd)=>{ if(n<2)return;
    ctx.strokeStyle=col;ctx.lineWidth=wd;ctx.beginPath();
    for(let i=0;i<n;i++){const y=pT+ih*(1-sc(a[(start+i)%TRN]));
      i?ctx.lineTo(px(i),y):ctx.moveTo(px(i),y);} ctx.stroke(); };
  const thr=starveThr();
  ctx.strokeStyle='rgba(46,124,123,.55)';ctx.lineWidth=1;ctx.setLineDash([4,3]);
  ctx.beginPath();const yt=pT+ih*(1-thr);ctx.moveTo(pL,yt);ctx.lineTo(w-pR,yt);ctx.stroke();
  ctx.setLineDash([]);
  line(tr.g,v=>v,'#2E7C7B',1.2);
  line(tr.resv,v=>v/mx,'#3E6C8C',1.3);
  line(tr.div,v=>v/mx,'#B3231C',1.4);
  line(tr.dens,v=>v,'#1B2A2E',1.8);
  ctx.strokeStyle='rgba(27,42,46,.6)';ctx.lineWidth=1;
  ctx.beginPath();ctx.moveTo(pL,pT);ctx.lineTo(pL,pT+ih);ctx.lineTo(w-pR,pT+ih);ctx.stroke();
  ctx.font='400 8.5px "IBM Plex Mono",monospace';ctx.fillStyle='rgba(92,110,112,1)';
  ctx.fillText('100%',2,pT+7);ctx.fillText('0',2,pT+ih+3);
  ctx.textAlign='right';ctx.fillStyle='#B3231C';ctx.fillText(mx.toFixed(0),w-3,pT+7);
  ctx.fillStyle='rgba(92,110,112,1)';
  ctx.fillText(tMax.toFixed(0)+' h',w-pR,h-3);
  ctx.textAlign='left';ctx.fillText(tMin.toFixed(0)+' h',pL,h-3);
}

function refresh(c){
  // reserve margin is a property of the NORMAL lineage only
  const need=needResv(), have=(c.nd+c.nv)/Math.max(1,c.norm);
  el('rNeed').textContent=(need*100).toFixed(1)+' %';
  const marg=have/need;
  el('rMarg').textContent=marg.toFixed(2)+' ×';
  el('rMarg').style.color = marg<1.15?'var(--div)':'';
  el('rDens').textContent=(c.dens*100).toFixed(0)+' %';
  el('rArea').textContent=c.aMean.toFixed(2);
  el('rT').innerHTML=(step*K.dtMin/60).toFixed(0)+' <small>h</small>';
  el('rN').textContent=c.cells;
  el('rDiff').textContent=c.nf;
  el('rResv').textContent=c.nd;
  el('rDiv').textContent=c.nv;
  el('rMob').innerHTML=(c.mob*100).toFixed(0)+' <small>%</small>';
  el('rShed').textContent=c.ns;
  el('rShedT').textContent=shedTotal;
  el('rStarve').textContent=starveTotal;
  el('rFree').textContent=c.free;
  el('rG').innerHTML=c.gmin.toFixed(2)+' <small>·C₀</small>';
  el('rThr').innerHTML=starveThr().toFixed(2)+' <small>·C₀</small>';
  el('rRevG').innerHTML=(revGAvg===null?'—':revGAvg.toFixed(2))+' <small>·g</small>';

  let wClosed=null;
  if(woundAt>=0){
    const rem=Math.max(0,c.free-woundBase);
    wClosed=1-rem/Math.max(1,woundN);
    if(wClosed>=0.995) woundAt=-1;
  }
  el('rWn').textContent = woundN?woundN:'—';
  el('rWc').innerHTML = (wClosed===null?'—':(wClosed*100).toFixed(0))+' <small>%</small>';
  el('rRev').textContent=c.rev;
  el('rRevR').textContent=c.revR;
  el('rRevT').textContent=revTotal;
  el('rRevL').textContent=revLost;
  el('rClone').innerHTML=c.clone+' <small>cells</small>';
  el('rWct').textContent=woundCount;
  el('rEst').textContent = revEstStep>=0
    ? 'at '+(revEstStep*K.dtMin/60).toFixed(0)+' h' : 'none';
  el('rEst').style.color = revEstStep>=0?'var(--div)':'';

  const md=el('mode'),nt=el('modeNote');
  if(wClosed!==null){
    md.className='mode warn'; md.textContent='Wound closing';
    nt.textContent=(wClosed*100).toFixed(0)+' % of '+woundN+
      ' nodes covered. Coverage proceeds by spreading and shift; division only restores density. '+
      'Mean cell area '+c.aMean.toFixed(2)+' nodes.';
  } else if(marg<1.05){
    md.className='mode warn'; md.textContent='Reserve at its limit';
    nt.textContent=(need*100).toFixed(1)+' % reserve required, '+(have*100).toFixed(1)+
      ' % available, '+(c.mob*100).toFixed(0)+' % mobilized. The sheet cannot hold density: '+
      (c.dens*100).toFixed(0)+' %, cells stretched '+c.aMean.toFixed(2)+'-fold. Nothing left to heal a wound with.';
  } else if(c.dens>0.93){
    md.className='mode'; md.textContent='Homeostasis';
    nt.textContent='Density '+(c.dens*100).toFixed(0)+' %, reserve margin '+marg.toFixed(2)+
      ' ×, '+(c.mob*100).toFixed(0)+' % mobilized. Shedding is balanced by division.';
  } else {
    md.className='mode'; md.textContent='Sheet converging';
    nt.textContent='Density '+(c.dens*100).toFixed(0)+' %, mean cell area '+
      c.aMean.toFixed(2)+'. Tension is above the setpoint; the reserve is pulling density back up.';
  }
}

/* ---------- main loop ---------- */
let last=null,lastCount=null,acc=0,tPrev=performance.now();
function loop(now){
  const dt=Math.min(.1,(now-tPrev)/1000); tPrev=now;
  if(S.running){
    acc+=dt*S.hps/(K.dtMin/60);
    let budget=320;   // must cover the top rung: 76.8 h/s at 0.05 h/step
    while(acc>=1&&budget-->0){ acc-=1; last=advance(); }
    if(acc>1) acc=0;
  }
  if(!last) last=count();
  lastCount=last;
  drawSheet(); drawTrace(); refresh(last);
  requestAnimationFrame(loop);
}

let rec=null,chunks=[],recStart=0,lastURL=null;
function pickMime(){
  const want=['video/webm;codecs=vp9','video/webm;codecs=vp8','video/webm','video/mp4'];
  if(typeof MediaRecorder==='undefined') return null;
  return want.find(t=>{try{return MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported(t)}catch(e){return false}})||null;
}
function stamp(){
  const d=new Date(), p=x=>String(x).padStart(2,'0');
  return d.getFullYear()+p(d.getMonth()+1)+p(d.getDate())+'-'+p(d.getHours())+p(d.getMinutes())+p(d.getSeconds());
}
